# Changelog

Every StoryForgeAI release, newest first. The five most recent also appear in
the [README](README.md#update-log); this file is the complete record and nothing
is ever removed from it.

The version shown in the app's footer is the top entry here.

| Version | Date | What changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2.11** | 2026-08-19 | **A pose is named, never adjusted.** The rule added in 2.06 for closing the distance between two figures asked for one of them to be lowered toward the other — phrased as a movement of a single body part, which is not something a still image can contain. A model has no way to move part of a body without moving everything attached to it, so the next render laid that figure flat alongside the other, and the detail it was meant to be reaching with was drawn attached to the wrong part of it: the exact failure 2.01 was written to stop. The requirement now says to close such a gap by naming the posture that produces it rather than asking a part to travel. "He kneels upright, back straight, shoulders high" describes a body a model can build; "his hips lowered" describes an adjustment it can only apply to all of him. |
| **2.10** | 2026-08-19 | **A head taking part in an action has to be told which way to turn, and told it toward the other person rather than the camera.** Version 2.04 stopped a head being aimed at the lens and at a point of contact simultaneously, which was right, and the fix that followed removed the direction altogether rather than correcting it. A head lying flat with no turn stated renders facing straight up — so with everything else finally correct, the second figure was placed exactly where it belonged and the contact still missed, passing across the cheek rather than meeting the face. The requirement on faces now says the turn must be stated, and stated toward the person it is serving: not a left, not a right, and not a direction from the camera, since a body lying down makes its own left and right the least reliable words in the prompt. |
| **2.09** | 2026-08-19 | **Equal weight, but not the same words.** Balancing two points of contact to the same length, as 2.08 requires, brought the second one back — and cost the first its fidelity. Written to match, the two ended up sharing most of their vocabulary, down to the same phrase for how the parts meet and the same closing detail. The render placed everything correctly and drew the two figures adjacent but not connected, which is what an echo of another description looks like. This is the duplicate-character rule one more level down: two things written in the same phrases are one description, and only one of them gets rendered properly. Contacts now have to be described to comparable weight *in their own vocabulary*. |
| **2.08** | 2026-08-19 | **Two points of contact in one frame have to be described to the same weight.** Describing a contact as a join rather than a proximity, as 2.07 requires, worked exactly as intended — and took the other one down with it. One grew to twice the length of the other and rendered perfectly; the shorter one, still a main clause and still describing its own join, was dropped from the picture entirely. The frame contract has known this rule for people since the cast instructions were written — a character described at four times the length of another renders twice and drops the other — and it turns out to hold for actions just as well. Where a frame holds more than one, they now have to be described to comparable length, because the model spends its attention in proportion to what it is given and the shortest description is the one that goes. |
| **2.07** | 2026-08-19 | **A contact is described as a join, not as two parts near each other.** With the pose finally putting the two figures within reach, the render came back with them adjacent and nothing connecting them — the model had drawn both things it was asked for and declined to join them. The contract already knew the better form and only ever used it in one place: requirement 4 has always described what the receiving side is doing *around* the other, which is a seal rather than a proximity. That form is now required of every contact. Name what the receiving part is doing around the other one, because a description of two things that merely happen to be adjacent is rendered as exactly that. |
| **2.06** | 2026-08-19 | **A contact the pose cannot reach is now the pose's problem, not the wording's.** Five rounds of fixes had a three-person frame rendering cleanly — the right number of people, anatomy where it belongs — with one point of contact missing every time. The prompt had been describing that contact more and more precisely, and the render kept dropping it, because the fault was never in the description. A figure kneeling upright carries its midsection about two feet above a head lying flat on a mattress. The gap is real, no phrasing closes it, and a model handed a contact across it renders the pose it was given and silently omits the contact — so the detail was not drawn at all. The frame contract now requires the pose to put the two parts within reach of each other, and names the arrangements that achieve it. |
| **2.05** | 2026-08-19 | **A body part is stated once, for the same reason a character is.** Version 1.88 established that naming a character in one sentence and describing them in another gives a model two subjects rather than one, because it has no coreference to join them. That is just as true one level down. A frame that referred to one figure's head in four separate places — its angle in the first, its expression in the second, what was against it in the third and a hand on it in the fourth — rendered two of the same figure sharing a single body, in a prompt that also stated "exactly three people are in frame" and carried the duplication guards. The requirement on faces now asks for the head in one clause: its angle, what is at it, and the expression together. This costs nothing and shortens the prompt, which is the opposite of the last three additions. |
| **2.04** | 2026-08-19 | **A head taking part in an action is no longer also aimed somewhere else.** With the action back in the main clause the renders came good — the right number of people, anatomy where it belongs — except for one point of contact that kept failing. The prompt was asking for two things that could not both be true: it aimed a figure's head at the camera and at a contact on the far side of it at the same time. The model kept the head angle and dropped the contact. The frame contract's requirement on faces now says the head is turned by the action wherever it takes part in one, and cannot be aimed anywhere else in the same breath — put the head where the contact needs it and let the eyes look wherever is left. Amended in place rather than added as a twelfth requirement, because two requirements that contradict each other are worse than one that is missing. |
| **2.03** | 2026-08-19 | **The action keeps the main clause, and staging is no longer described in the camera's terms.** 2.02 asked for each body's position in the frame, and the next render answered it perfectly: three people correctly placed, correctly lit, and not touching each other anywhere. Eleven requirements compete for one prompt and the model settles that competition by weighting the main clause — so staging in front with the action trailing behind as a phrase returns exactly what was asked for and nothing that matters. The contract now states outright that the action is the main clause of its own sentence and everything else is arranged around it. The staging requirement is also cut to one clause and forbidden from talking about the photograph: "in profile to the lens" and "nothing blocks the view" describe a picture rather than a scene and a model can build neither, in the same way it cannot build "the frame is free of". Where something has to be visible the prompt now says what is visible instead. |
| **2.02** | 2026-08-19 | **An action now has to be staged where the camera can see it, and a three-person frame has to name the arrangement for every point of contact.** Anchoring anatomy to its owner's body in 2.01 fixed the malformations — and revealed what it had been hiding. A figure described as kneeling "beside her head, hips level with her face" satisfies that wording by kneeling *behind* the head, which is anatomically fine, geometrically impossible for the action described, and edge-on to the lens: the render came back correct in every other respect with the contact simply not in the picture. The frame contract gains an eleventh requirement covering where each body sits in the frame, which way it faces, and that no point of contact is screened by a body between it and the camera. The first requirement now also asks for a named arrangement covering every point of contact rather than one, because three people joined by hand-assembled prepositions leave the model to invent the rest — a named position carries a whole geometry that "beside her head" does not. Applies when prompts are next rewritten. |
| **2.01** | 2026-08-19 | **Anatomy is now anchored to the body it grows from, and two people who look alike are told apart.** A frame that placed a body part against another figure, without ever saying whose body it belonged to or where that body was, rendered the part growing out of the wrong person entirely — a model has no anatomy prior strong enough to supply the missing half. The frame contract gains a tenth requirement: state where each participant's own body is, so every part named is anchored to its owner. Frames and clips also carry a guard for a part attached in the wrong place, which is a different failure from a detached one and was covered by nothing — folded on Krea and FLUX as a positive statement that every part of each body sits where it anatomically belongs. Separately, two people written as "a heavy-set man in his 40s" and "the second heavy-set man" are one description as far as a model is concerned, which is how a three-person scene came back with four people in it; prompt agents are now told to give each person one detail that separates them. The anatomy guard applies to existing projects immediately; the two instructions apply when prompts are next rewritten. |
| **2.00** | 2026-08-19 | **You can now keep your pinned model on every scene instead of losing it to the carried frame.** Warning you that a pin would only render the first scene, as 1.99 does, left two poor answers: change the model, or give up seam matching entirely by cutting between scenes. There was a third all along, available only as a per-scene checkbox fifteen times over — withhold the picture and let the prompt and the face swap carry the scene. The join never depended on the reference: a scene's end frame becomes the next scene's start frame regardless, which is what actually makes the cut invisible. Project settings now offers it once, for the whole project: **Show it the carried frame** for the strongest wardrobe and set continuity, or **Prompt and face swap only** to keep the pinned model and its LoRAs rendering every scene. Choose the second when a particular model's look matters more than the set matching exactly frame to frame — with it, a pin like Krea 2 Turbo renders all fifteen scenes rather than one. |
| **1.99** | 2026-08-19 | **A model that takes no reference images now warns you before it renders one scene and hands the rest to another model.** Under the default scene continuity every scene after the first renders its end frame against the frame it carried over, and that frame is a reference image — so a pin like Krea 2 Turbo or Z-Image, which accept none, runs scene 1 and is substituted for all fourteen scenes after it, dropping the pinned family's LoRAs on the way. The warning added in 1.92 asked only whether the character library was sending photographs, so a project with that switched off saw no warning at all and found out from the run log. The settings screen now counts a carried frame as what it is, says the pin will only render the first scene, and names the two things that actually fix it: pin the Identity Edit variant of the same family, which keeps its LoRAs, or set scene continuity to Cut. Nothing about which model renders has changed — only whether you are told in advance. |
| **1.98** | 2026-08-18 | **A model that cannot be told what to leave out is no longer told what to leave out.** On Krea and FLUX the negative prompt is discarded, so exclusions are folded into the positive prompt as the thing to render instead — and anything with no such alternative fell through to a closing sentence reading "the frame is free of …". That sentence names the very things it is trying to prevent, to a model that cannot process negation, which is the failure 1.86 identified and fixed for duplication guards only. It applied to everything else all along. Two men the prompt describes once as "heavy-set" were arriving alongside a clause naming *Athletic physique, Toned abdominal muscles, Lean muscle definition* and *Muscular arms* — written to protect a different character's build — and rendered muscular in every take that carried it and heavy-set in the one that did not. Across one fifteen-scene project between ten and thirty-five exclusions per frame were being folded in this way, including a character's entire stored list of twenty-one body descriptions. Terms that can be expressed as something to render still are; the rest are dropped and counted in the run log, so the loss is visible. Exclusions reach every model that acts on a negative prompt exactly as before. |
| **1.97** | 2026-08-18 | **The scene now leads its own prompt, and two exclusions that were working against it are gone.** Rendered live at a fixed seed against identical reference images, a scene's own prompt beat the same prompt as StoryForge composed it — so the appended text was costing more than it bought. Three faults behind that. Sixty words of framing boilerplate were being inserted between the shot size and the first person in the picture, on the reasoning that anything later would be lost behind the appended cast sheet; since 1.89 that sheet is usually skipped, so the note now goes at the end and is a third shorter. It also said "no one is cropped", which the routine that recovers traits from a prompt harvested as a thing to avoid, folding `is cropped` and `is left as` back into the render instruction — two fragments of English in the highest-weight exclusion clause of every carried frame. And an exclusion is now recognised as one the prompt is asking for even with a word in the middle: two men described "with short cropped hair" were going out alongside an exclusion reading "Short hair", pulling the render both ways on the one feature that told them apart. |
| **1.96** | 2026-08-18 | **You can now read the prompt that actually reaches Wan2GP, without rendering anything.** `npm run render:preview -- <projectId> [sceneNumber]` composes every keyframe job a batch would submit and prints it: the resolved model, seed, steps, reference image paths, the conditional-image mode, and the finished prompt. That text is not what the scene card says. The card is the input to a chain of appenders — cast sheet, project look, carry and match instructions — and on Krea and FLUX the exclusions are folded into it as well, against whichever model resolves at render time rather than the one pinned. Diagnosing a wrong frame by reading the card was therefore close to guesswork, and the composed text existed nowhere a person could see it. The preview also reports, for every scene, whether its opening frame is rendered or inherited and which seam broke the chain — the answer to "why did this scene not start where the last one ended". Nothing is submitted and nothing is written. |
| **1.95** | 2026-08-18 | **A carried-over frame is now asked for the edit, not just told what to keep.** When a scene starts from the previous scene's ending, that frame is handed to the end-frame render so faces, wardrobe and hair hold across the join — and the instruction sent with it listed only what to preserve. Every model that can accept a reference image is an instruction-following edit checkpoint, and those answer the question they are asked: asked to preserve and nothing else, they preserved the composition too. Two characters scripted to walk from a doorway toward a bed came back at the doorway, unchanged. The instruction now names the change first, states outright that the location, framing, camera position and action come from the scene rather than the picture, keeps the identity and wardrobe carry-over, and closes by asking for the change to be integrated. This is an improvement rather than a guarantee — **Match the carried-over frame** is still the reliable lever for a scene whose ending must show something the reference does not contain. Nothing needs rewriting: the instruction is composed at render time. |
| **1.94** | 2026-08-18 | **Clips now get the same exclusions their keyframes do.** Every correction the image pass has accumulated — dropping a term the agent scoped to one character by name, dropping a character's stored negative on a shot holding other people, dropping an exclusion the prompt itself asks for, moving a "no X" out of the positive prompt where it does nothing, and guarding a crowded frame against duplication — was applied only to keyframes. The clip was handed whatever the agent originally wrote, untouched, so the same "heavy-set" that had been stripped from a keyframe was still steering every body in the video built from it. Both passes now go through one composer, so neither can drift from the other again. A clip's population is read from the frame it opens on, since a video prompt is deliberately told not to re-describe what that frame already shows. Nothing is stored differently and no prompt needs rewriting — this is composed at render time from the terms already on the scene. |
| **1.93** | 2026-08-18 | **A crowded frame is no longer asked for as a close-up, and a participant is no longer dressed and undressed in the same prompt.** A three-person scene opened "Close-Up" while naming people at opposite ends of one body — a frame with nowhere to put them — and the render dropped the second figure entirely, substituting an unidentifiable object where a body part should have been. That is what a model does when it is asked for anatomy the shot has no room for. Prompt agents are now told which shot sizes hold how many people, and a prompt that ignores it is widened to fit before it renders. The same prompt also dressed a participant in a t-shirt and cargo trousers *and* asserted the opposite: the repair had only ever appended the corrective clause, leaving the garment sentence standing, and the garment sent to the negative prompt was then dropped again for appearing in the positive — the fix cancelled itself at both ends. The outfit now comes off the sentence that invented it. Frames of this kind also carry four new exclusions for the substitutions a model reaches for when it cannot resolve anatomy — stray objects, detached body parts, obscured detail, censoring — each written so it survives the fold on Krea and FLUX as something to render rather than something to avoid. |
| **1.92** | 2026-08-17 | **The image model picker now says when your pin is not the model being used.** A project that sends character reference photographs cannot render on a model that does not accept them, so StoryForgeAI substitutes one that can — deliberate, documented two panels further down the same screen, and completely invisible where it mattered. The picker went on showing the pinned choice as though jobs were running on it, so a project pinned to Z-Image Turbo was rendering every keyframe on Flux 2 Klein with nothing on screen to say so. The pin now carries a notice naming the model actually used and why, with the two ways out: pin a model marked ✓ refs, or switch the likeness setting to description and face swap. The same notice appears for a pin WanGP no longer lists. Nothing about model selection has changed — only whether the screen admits to it. |
| **1.91** | 2026-08-17 | **Changing the video model no longer makes you rewrite the image prompts too.** Swap the pinned video model on an existing project and the Storyboard screen warns that the clip prompts were written for the old family — which is true, and the only thing that is true. The button offered to fix it rewrote both prompt passes, so a fifteen-scene project paid thirty model calls to change fifteen prompts, and the start and end frame prompts came back rewritten from scratch, taking any wording typed by hand with them. A video model has nothing to say about how a still frame should be described, so the warning's button now runs the video prompt agent alone: one call per scene instead of two, and the frame prompts are left exactly as they were. **Rewrite all video prompts** sits beside **Rewrite all prompts** above the scene list for the same job outside the warning. |
| **1.90** | 2026-08-16 | **The headcount is a short sentence again, and nothing else is allowed into it.** The line reading "Exactly three people are in frame" is what stops an image model drawing somebody twice, and it only works while it is short enough to register. Since 1.87 told prompt agents to describe each character in the sentence that places them, one agent picked the headcount sentence to do it in — producing a single sixty-word sentence that asserted the count, described a character's hair and clothing, and placed her asleep in a bed, with forty-five words standing between "Exactly three people" and the end of the sentence. The count was buried and her position in the frame was demoted to a trailing clause. Prompt agents are now told the headcount carries counts and role nouns only, and that each person is described and placed in a sentence of their own. |
| **1.89** | 2026-08-16 | **The continuity sheet is no longer appended to a prompt that already describes the character.** 1.87 made the appended sheet a fallback, to be skipped wherever the prompt agent had described a character inline as instructed. In practice it was never skipped: the check compared the prompt against the character's entire library record, while the sheet only ever prints a truncated opening slice of it. One live character runs to a hundred and fifty words of build, skin, nails and jewellery, so a prompt reproducing every word the sheet would have printed still scored below the threshold and got the sheet anyway — a second description of the same person, which is the thing that makes an image model draw her twice. The check now measures against the text the sheet would actually print. Worth saying plainly: the model's own output had been correct since 1.88; only what was appended to it was wrong. |
| **1.88** | 2026-08-16 | **A character's description must now share the sentence that places them, and the instruction shows what that looks like.** 1.87 asked prompt agents to describe each character inline instead of relying on an appended sheet. The first storyboard written under it complied only halfway: it placed the sleeping figure in one sentence and then described her in a second, opening with the character's name and a full run of physical attributes. That is still two mentions of one person, and an image model that has never heard of her reads two mentions as two people — which is the whole defect. The instruction now carries a worked example of the right shape and the wrong one side by side, and states plainly that each character is mentioned exactly once. |
| **1.87** | 2026-08-16 | **A character is now described once, in the sentence that places them.** Render prompts named a pinned character and left their appearance to a continuity sheet appended at the end — and the prompt agents were explicitly instructed to work that way, on the reasoning that two descriptions of one person make an image model draw two. The reasoning was right; what it missed is that the appended sheet *is* the second description. An image model has never heard of these characters, so a name in one sentence and an appearance in another do not refer to the same person: they are two subjects, and it draws two. A three-person bedroom scene came back with a generic woman asleep in the bed where the prose had put her, and a second, fully on-model one sitting up in the foreground wearing every item the sheet listed. Prompt agents are now asked to describe each pinned character inline, in the same clause that places them in the shot, in roughly twenty-five words — the rule that already applied to everyone else present. That also ends the imbalance where one character carried twice the description of the other two combined, which is the documented reason a model draws that character twice and drops another. The sheet is still appended for any character a prompt only names, so no description is ever lost. Existing prompts are unchanged until they are rewritten. |
| **1.86** | 2026-08-16 | **A crowded frame is no longer asked for "a single subject", or handed the word "twins".** Krea 2 and FLUX discard a negative prompt, so every exclusion is folded into the positive prompt as the thing to render instead — and the guards added to a shot holding two or more people came through that fold backwards. "Duplicated person" and "extra limbs" were rewritten as "a single correctly formed subject", which is right for a portrait and flatly wrong in a scene that asked for three people, while "twins", "cloned face" and "the same face twice" had no rewrite at all and arrived as "the frame is free of the same face twice, twins" — naming duplication inside a prompt that cannot express absence, which is the exact mistake the fold exists to prevent. A three-person bedroom scene came back with the same woman drawn twice and neither of the two men in it. A duplication guard is now never spelled out as an absence, and on a frame that states its population they collapse into a stated count of distinct people; a guard about a body rather than a headcount keeps its wording. The folding itself was re-tested rather than assumed: the same tomato rendered through Krea 2 at a fixed seed, with and without an exclusion reading "red", is red both times. |
| **1.85** | 2026-08-16 | **A prompt the model wrote is no longer labelled "Deterministic".** The badge beside each prompt reports who wrote it, and it could say the template did when the model had written every word. The label showing how much of a batch came from the model needs a per-item count, and a single scene's prompt has none — one scene is one call — so every prompt the checks had adjusted fell through to the label meant for work the model never touched. On the project this was found in, not one image prompt was actually deterministic: thirteen came straight from the model and twenty-four were model-written with an adjustment. Those now read "Hybrid". The accompanying explanation no longer calls a single prompt a "set" either. Nothing about how prompts are produced has changed — only what the badge says about them, which was the one thing it exists to get right. |
| **1.84** | 2026-08-16 | **Two prompt checks stop reporting faults that were not faults.** Counting the outcome of every check across a rewritten fifteen-scene storyboard showed most remaining complaints were the checks themselves. A scene was marked wrong whenever its two frames held different numbers of people — which rejected a scene scripted as "the two men walk across the floor as they approach the bed" and another as "walk towards the door … the empty room", both deliberate. That check now fires only where the closing frame *gains* someone and no clip exists to walk them in, matching what the renderer already decides for itself. Separately, the check on whether a prompt still describes its scene compares a third of the scene card's words, so it cannot tell a rewording from an omission: it rejected "pulling the blankets away to reveal her skin" against a card reading "whip the blankets away, exposing her skin". It still asks for a rewrite and still marks the scene for review, but it no longer pastes the card over a prompt that already says the same thing better. |
| **1.83** | 2026-08-16 | **A scene that opens on the previous scene's closing frame is no longer marked wrong for doing so.** On a continuous take that frame is inherited, never rendered, and the prompt writer is told to open at what the picture already shows — but the check on whether a prompt describes its scene was applied to it anyway, so the most faithful possible opening was rejected for not containing this scene's action, and the patch then pasted that action into it. This was the single most common complaint recorded across a fifteen-scene storyboard. The check now applies to an inherited opening's closing frame only, which is where the action actually has to land, and still applies to both frames of any scene that renders its own opening. |
| **1.82** | 2026-08-16 | **A scene where someone changes out of their clothes is no longer treated as an intimate encounter.** The check that asks such a frame to name anatomy, contact and a position was triggered by a single word appearing anywhere on the scene card — so a close-up of a character undressing was rejected for naming none of them, which is a demand that scene cannot meet. The prompt was correct; the rejection then patched it by appending the card's whole action, folding a three-step process into a single frozen frame. Those three checks now apply only where the card actually reaches an encounter. The separate check on clothing the model invented still applies wherever a card leaves someone uncovered, so nothing is given back a costume it was not meant to have. |
| **1.81** | 2026-08-16 | **Keyframe prompts stop describing time passing, and the direction you pick now reaches the story.** A still image has no camera move and no duration in it, but prompts were being written with both — the template literally appended "slow push-in begins from here" to every opening frame it produced, and the check meant to catch that language only ever ran on scenes flagged as adult. It now runs on every project, and rejects a named camera move, a stated duration or a transition while still allowing a pose caught mid-action, which is a legitimate thing for a frame to show. The instruction to the prompt writer no longer calls the two frames "the same moment seconds apart", which is a contradiction; they are described as two frozen instants, with actions to be written as poses, contact and visible consequences. Three planning faults are fixed alongside: choosing a creative direction and then running the Director produced a story arc written as though nothing had been chosen, and that arc was reused from then on; the tradeoffs a direction accepts were being passed downstream as things to avoid, working against the direction you picked; and a story arc returning fewer emotional beats than segments left every later batch of scenes with no emotional direction at all. Per-scene director and camera plans that skip segments are now reported instead of stored silently. |
| **1.80** | 2026-08-16 | **LoRAs now work with Krea 2, and with any model that supports them without advertising the setting.** StoryForgeAI decided whether a model could take LoRAs by looking for the LoRA fields in its settings schema. Those fields describe the controls Wan2GP draws for a model, not what its pipeline actually accepts — Krea 2 RAW and Turbo, both Identity Edit variants, and LTX 2.5 all load LoRAs happily while declaring none of them, so picking one made every scene fail with "accepts no LoRAs" before rendering anything. It now reads the model's own capability flag, which Wan2GP publishes for every model in the catalogue. That also retires the hand-written exception previously needed for MiniMax H3 reference mode, which publishes the same flag. A model claiming neither the fields nor the capability still refuses loudly, rather than rendering something plausible with no LoRA applied and nothing to explain it. |
| **1.79** | 2026-08-16 | **A model you download while StoryForgeAI is running now shows up without restarting it.** The list of Wan2GP models was read once and kept for as long as the app stayed running. Whether a model is installed is not fixed, though — it changes the moment Wan2GP finishes fetching the weights — so a model downloaded and used successfully in Wan2GP went on being hidden from every picker here, with Wan2GP reporting it ready the whole time. Reproduced on the new Krea 2 RAW checkpoints. The catalogue is now re-read at most once a minute, matching the LoRA list, so a newly installed model appears after a page reload. Model details and settings are still cached as before, so the refresh costs one call rather than one per model. |
| **1.78** | 2026-08-15 | **A character's exclusions no longer fight the rest of the frame.** The negative prompt saved against a character in the library describes one person, but a negative prompt has no addressee — so a list added to keep one character slim was steering every body in the shot, including people the same prompt describes as heavy. Combined with the world bible's own opposing rules, a three-person frame could be forbidden every build there is, which is why those renders came back malformed. Character exclusions are now dropped from any frame holding more than one person, where they cannot be aimed, and kept where the frame holds one. Separately, any exclusion the positive prompt is actually asking for is removed before the job is sent, because a term on both sides of one render is a contradiction the sampler settles by averaging. Two smaller corrections: the scene that changes someone's clothes on screen no longer has the "everyone is uncovered" clause forced onto its opening frame, which is meant to show the clothes still on; and a scene whose two frames state different numbers of people is now sent back for a rewrite, instead of gaining or losing someone between its opening and closing instant. These apply to existing projects at render time — no prompt rewrite needed. |
| **1.77** | 2026-08-14 | **Keyframe prompts no longer repeat themselves.** A prompt that was already correct could still be flagged and patched, because the check deciding whether it described its scene concretely recognised only a short list of phrasings and rejected well-written prose that said the same thing in other words. The patch then appended the scene card's action, that action's sentences a second time, and the story beat's reworded version of the same event — so the image model was told one thing three times and weighted it three times, which the storyboard screen reported as "a sentence is repeated". The check now reads how scenes are actually written, including plain spatial staging rather than only named arrangements. The patch will not restate anything the prompt already carries, in the same words or as a paraphrase of them, and it no longer attempts to fix shortcomings a template cannot fix — those are flagged for review instead of padded. Existing prompts keep their repetition until the affected scenes are rewritten from the storyboard screen. |
| **1.76** | 2026-08-14 | **LTX video prompts now follow LTX's published guide, and exclusions no longer vanish on LTX 2.5.** The new `ltx2_25_*` checkpoints are recognised automatically and inherit the LTX prompt profile, so pinning one needs no extra setup.
